# Path Planning Based on Improved Continuous Ant Colony Optimization for UAVs in Complex Environments [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction
In recent years, Unmanned Aerial Systems (UAS) have been widely used in various civilian fields such as agricultural monitoring and logistics transportation. As a key component of UAS, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) rely on reliable path planning algorithms to assist in completing tasks. Path planning is a critical technology for autonomous flight of UAVs, requiring high intelligence and flexibility to determine the optimal feasible paths for each UAV. Due to the autonomy and complexity of UAVs, navigating through continuous search spaces in partially unknown environments presents a challenging problem of ensuring that planned paths are collision-free and feasible.
Generally speaking, path planning problems can be categorized into global path planning based on static environments and local path planning based on dynamic environments [1]. For global path planning, researchers have made significant efforts in recent years and proposed various algorithms, including node-based methods, sampling-based methods, potential field methods, and evolutionary computing algorithms (EC) [5]. However, when facing large search spaces, node-based methods often consume more memory. In sampling-based methods, environmental sampling is required. Insufficient sampling can lead to poor path performance, while excessive sampling increases the computational burden of the entire planning process, reducing the real-time efficiency of path planning. In potential field methods, when the robot's distance from obstacles exceeds the range of the obstacle's influence, the robot is not affected by the repulsion potential field, making it easy to fall into local minima. For more information on these global path planning algorithms, please refer to Section 2.
As an important subset of EC, the group intelligence algorithm is a heuristic search algorithm based on collective behaviors of agents. Its core lies in utilizing intelligent agents within a cluster to exchange information and collaborate for optimization, demonstrating superior performance in solving global path planning problems and other non-deterministic polynomial (NP) complete problems [6]. Consequently, particle swarm optimization (PSO) and ant colony optimization (ACO) have been extensively studied by scholars since their inception. They have been successfully applied in many fields of global path planning, such as unmanned aerial vehicle trajectory planning [9], [10], [11], [12], robot navigation [13], [14], [15], and logistics transportation [16].
ACO, one of the most famous algorithms for group intelligence, simulates the process of information exchange and path selection by ants while searching for food. It possesses strong global search capabilities and excellent group collaboration characteristics. Compared to other algorithms, this makes ACO promising in finding high-quality solutions in complex environments. Most improvements to ACO focus on two aspects: hybridization and multi-strategy enhancement. Hybridization [17] combines the advantages of ACO with those of other algorithms to achieve enhanced performance. Multi-strategy methods [18] enable Ants to compete and cooperate, explore the solution space, and avoid getting trapped in local optima. Although these improvements help ACO avoid static obstacles and find feasible paths in drone path planning, there are still limitations in most practical tasks: (1) The algorithm is prone to finding poor quality solutions in complex path models with multiple constraints, such as 3D environments. Additionally, convergence speed is also a challenge for ACO and its variants; (2) Most existing problem models typically simplify 2D or 3D environments based on discrete search spaces, which may not fully simulate the complexity and dynamic changes of the real world. When the 3D environment is partially unknown, the algorithm cannot effectively find feasible paths. Moreover, when the search space is continuous, it increases the number of possible paths, thereby reducing search efficiency. Therefore, designing an algorithm that combines ACO with local path planning algorithms in complex 3D environments with continuous search spaces is necessary to enable unobstructed flight of drones in the presence of static and moving obstacles and improve flight quality.
To address the issue of path planning for unmanned aerial vehicles in dynamic environments, scholars have proposed various local path planning methods. These include the Circle-RRT (circle-RRT) [19], Optimized Q-Learning (O-QL) [20], Improved Reinforcement Learning (IRL) [21], and Dynamic Window Approach (DWA) [22]. Compared to other local path planning algorithms, DWA takes into account the kinematic constraints of the robot, is easy to implement, and has high computational efficiency, making it highly suitable for integration with global planning algorithms to solve complex path planning problems.
Based on the above content, we proposed a method that improves state transition probability, random walk strategy, and adaptive waypoint repair (ACOSRAR) to solve complex 3D path planning problems in continuous search spaces. Additionally, we successfully integrated dynamic window approach (DWA) with ACOSRA to address unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) path planning problems in partially unknown scenarios with static and moving obstacles.
The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
•  
A new variant of continuous ant colony optimization with improved state transition probability, random walk strategy, and adaptive waypoint repair method is proposed for global path planning of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) to ensure collision-free flight and minimize the cost of reaching the destination.
•  
A new adaptive waypoint repair method is proposed, which combines the single-axis repair method with the adaptive selection. This method effectively solves the path planning problem in complex environments with multiple obstacles and enhances the feasibility of generated paths and the search efficiency of the algorithm.
•  
The effectiveness of the proposed algorithm was verified by comparing its results with those of competing algorithms that generated complex scenarios using real digital elevation models (DEM) maps. We also effectively integrated the dynamic window approach (DWA) with ACOSRA to solve the drone path planning problem in partially unknown scenarios with static and moving obstacles.

Conclusion
In this paper, we introduce a new path planning algorithm R, named ACOSRA, aimed at solving complex path planning problems. It employs an improved state transition probability, random walk strategy, and adaptive waypoint repair method to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of path planning. Firstly, it improves the search process efficiency by enhancing the state transition probability, thereby promoting rapid convergence. Additionally, it combines the random walk strategy to generate steps with different distributions. This feature prevents the algorithm from being trapped in local optima and increases the likelihood of discovering new solutions. Finally, the adaptive waypoints-repair method is used to correct and optimize the paths found.
The effectiveness of ACOSRA was tested in 9 different real DEM map scenarios. Compared to most existing research on drone path planning in 3D environments, our work enables drones to navigate horizontally to avoid obstacles and facilitate vertical obstacle crossing, significantly improving flight quality. Finally, we successfully integrated DWA with ACOSRA to jointly solve the path planning problem for drones with static and moving obstacles. Experimental results show that our proposed algorithm ACOSRAR can obtain high-quality solutions in various environments, effectively addressing the three-dimensional path planning problem for drones in complex environments.
Looking forward to future research directions, although our current algorithms are promising for providing effective path planning solutions for individual drones in dynamic 3D environments, certain limitations still need to be addressed. One major limitation is our assumption that moving obstacles maintain a constant speed and trajectory. In reality, obstacles may exhibit unpredictable behaviors such as speed changes and sudden direction shifts, which could challenge the adaptability of the algorithm. Therefore, one focus of future research will be developing algorithms that can robustly handle moving obstacles with random speeds and frequent changes in movement. Additionally, our goal is to expand our work to support multi-drone systems, which introduces additional complexity in terms of coordination and collision avoidance. This will require the design of more complex coordination mechanisms and collision avoidance strategies to ensure safe and efficient operation of multiple drones within a shared airspace environment. Furthermore, we see great potential in integrating our algorithms with other advanced technologies, such as machine learning. By leveraging machine learning techniques, we can enable the algorithm to learn from past experiences and adapt to changing environmental conditions, thereby improving the accuracy and efficiency of path planning.
